How the path is planned

What it does

  • Goes level by level from your level to your goal and builds a list to follow: where to train, which quests to do, how long each takes.
  • Your character at each level is the Character Builder's default: best weapon you can use, AP and SP spent by the Auto buttons.
  • Class guides (default: build + training maps, levels 1–70): uses meowdb's Warrior, Magician, Bowman, Thief and beginner guides instead:
    • their SP order and AP per level (each level-up is listed under the step it happens in), their shop weapons and gear, free advancement weapons (Beginner's Garnier), Arrow Blow over Double Shot;
    • their Training Advisor maps for each level band (checked for sure hits, 2-hit kills, danger and the walk to town, which this model doesn't check), with the model's fastest map shown when it differs. "Build only" keeps the build but lets the model pick maps;
    • citizenship town at 12, and Wolbi stars or Bronze arrows from 25 (they unlock at citizenship grade 3: level 22 plus 2,000 contribution; reaching it by 25 is an assumption);
    • future Bandits level with a claw and Lucky Seven ("Sindit") and reset their SP at 40 (Cash Shop, 7,000 NX) to switch to daggers, as the Bandit guide does;
    • levels 30–70: each 2nd-job guide's SP order, AP rules, weapons every 10 levels and Training Advisor maps every 5 levels. Their Forgotten Hollow picks are skipped (not at launch). Above 70 the leftover SP and the weapon are picked by the model.
  • Quests + grinding: does each quest as it unlocks and grinds in between. Each quest shows how its EXP per hour compares with grinding; tick "Only quests faster than grinding" to keep just those.
  • Most rewards: every quest that pays mesos or items, Must do picks first, then lowest level and highest reward value. Items with no known source aren't timed ("+ other items").
  • Reward value = quest mesos + what its items sell to NPCs (pick-one: the best; random: the average). That's a floor; rated items sell for far more to players.
  • Quests only: every quest it can time, lowest level first, until they run out. Grinding only: maps only.
  • Item drops: drop rates aren't in the game files, so the plan assumes 30% per kill by default; slide it from 10% to 35% to test better or worse luck. At 30%, a quest needing 20 Octopus Legs costs about 67 Octopus kills.
  • Collecting ahead: while grinding, drops for quests up to 5 levels ahead go in your bag, even before you accept the quest (assuming these drop without the quest, as guides advise saving them). Maps get credit for the farming those drops save later, so the plan may pick a map a bit lower in EXP/hr when its drops pay off. Grind steps list what you collect; quest steps show how many are already saved.
  • Valuable quests (Sauna Robe, Icarus Cape, Work Gloves...) are marked with their rating and why players rate it. The ones the plan can't time (items with no known source, or skipped by your mode) appear as side tasks at the level their chain starts. Picks come from community guides; see the Quest Database.
  • Quests more than 5 levels under your current level count as already done or skipped. Quests with no EXP are skipped unless a later quest needs them.
  • It stays on its current map unless another one is more than 10% better, so you don't hop maps every level.
  • Grind rows show the map's danger for your character (one hit as a share of Max HP, no armor, and how often it lands) and the portals to potions.
  • If it's crowded: each grind row lists up to 2 backup maps whose monsters average within 5 levels of the pick and give at least 60% of its EXP/hr (same model, from the game files). The percentage is their EXP/hr against the pick; a backup can be faster when the pick is a guide map or wins on quest drops or a shorter walk. With class guides on, backups can be maps the guide didn't pick.
  • Mesos: monster meso drops (player reports on meowdb, ~2 per monster level where nobody has reported) and quest mesos, against weapons at NPC shop prices and potions and ammo while grinding. The potion bill assumes skills on every attack; quest potions are taken off it. Selling loot isn't counted.
  • Copy link makes a link that opens this exact plan.
  • Party: grinding uses your share of the map plus the party EXP bonus (see Where to train). Quests stay solo. The Kerning Party Quest shows up at 21 as an optional step.
  • Goes up to the level-100 cap with 2nd job (3rd job isn't at launch). EXP per level is the launch client's table (levels 90-99 cost 2.1× the level before).

Times are estimates

  • Grinding times come from the "Where to train" model (solo unless you pick a party).
  • Average player takes 1.5× the model time, Relaxed 2×: crowded maps, potions, breaks. Check: at 1.5× the plan takes about 3–4.5 h for 10→20 and 9–10 h for 20→30; meowdb reports COT2 testers took 3–4 h and 8–9 h.
  • Travel counts: every move between maps is its own step with the route (walk, cab), so you can tick it off. The plan knows where you are. A quest means walking to its NPC, to the closest good map for each monster it needs, and back. Grinding maps far from you lose points for the walk. Crossing one map ≈ 30 s, a cab between towns (Lith Harbor, Henesys, Ellinia, Kerning City, Perion, Sleepywood) ≈ 45 s, talking to an NPC ≈ 1 min (30 s for the same NPC again). All guesses.
  • Quest kills also wait on the map's respawns: few of a monster on a map means slower kills there. Kill EXP from quests counts.
  • AoE build (default): each level's character uses its best multi-target attack when the job has one: Slash Blast, Arrow Bomb, Iron Arrow, Thunder Bolt, Poison Breath (Power Knockback is skipped: it pushes monsters away). Thieves, 1st job Bowmen and 1st job Magicians have none, so they stay single target (Double Shot's 2 arrows split between targets, so it does the same total damage either way). How many monsters a cast hits comes from each map's spawn points on the same platform within the skill's reach (75% alive), capped at its target count: an estimate shown as "hits ~2.4 per cast".
  • Weapons: when the plan's character gets a better weapon, a step says where to get it: NPC shop and price (meowdb's shop list), crafting recipe (game files), quest reward, or monster drops (meowdb: player reports, else the old MapleSEA drop list it says Classic likely shares). The plan only picks weapons you can get at launch this way.
  • Below level 10 you're a Beginner: basic attack with the Wooden Club, the Razor from level 5, and all AP in your future job's main stat, as meowdb's beginner guide recommends.
  • Maple Island runs first in every mode: every island quest in order, island maps when a quest needs a higher level, then Shanks' ship to Lith Harbor at level 7+ (300 mesos, one way). meowdb: the island quests take you to about level 8. Untick "Still on Maple Island" if you've left.

Left out

  • Quests needing items with no known drop source (NPC-made or unclear). They're listed under the plan, and quests that come after them in a chain are skipped too.
  • Event, crafting, repeatable and Citizenship quests; Casey's Omok and Match Cards sets (very rare drops); Orbis, El Nath and Forgotten Hollow.
  • Buying gear. 3rd job (not at launch): the plan keeps 2nd job up to the level-100 cap.
